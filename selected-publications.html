Can shading drive the transition from black band disease to a cyanobacterial patch–like stage in Montipora corals?

2024 MARINE POLLUTION BULLETIN

Shallow corals acclimate to mesophotic depths while maintaining their heat tolerance against ongoing climate change

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2024). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 209, 117277.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Corals transplanted from shallow reefs to 40 m depth acclimated to the cooler, low-light environment while retaining strong thermal tolerance. Mesophotic corals showed higher thermal thresholds, delayed bleaching and less physiological decline during heat stress, supporting the potential of deeper habitats as refugia for shallow corals.

2024 CORAL REEFS

Susceptibility of Acropora tenuis to consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Morita M* (2024). Coral Reefs, 43, 4.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Repeated bleaching-level heat stress progressively reduced coral resilience. After three consecutive years of thermal stress, survival declined to only 20%, accompanied by reductions in symbiont density and photosynthetic efficiency. The findings demonstrate that repeated heatwaves can progressively increase coral susceptibility rather than generating unlimited acclimation.

2023 MARINE POLLUTION BULLETIN

Plasticity of shallow reef corals across a depth gradient

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 197, 115060.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Shallow corals displayed substantial plasticity when transplanted across a broad depth gradient. Several species survived and acclimated to markedly different light environments, although responses differed among species. These differences reveal species-specific limits to depth expansion and the potential role of phenotypic plasticity in coral persistence under environmental change.

2023 FRONTIERS IN MARINE SCIENCE

Variability in thermal stress thresholds of corals across depths

Tavakoli-Kolour P, Sinniger F, Morita M, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Frontiers in Marine Science, 10, 1210662.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Thermal thresholds varied among coral species and depths, challenging the assumption that mesophotic corals are universally more heat tolerant. The findings suggest that mesophotic habitats may buffer corals against lower levels of thermal stress, while severe heat exposure can still cause bleaching and mortality.

2023 MARINE POLLUTION BULLETIN

Response of resistant larvae of the coral Acropora tenuis to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Harii S* (2023). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 192, 115060.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Thermal conditioning during the larval stage did not provide lasting thermal tolerance in juvenile corals. Although larvae were exposed to elevated temperatures, their later juvenile survival under heat stress was not improved, revealing an important limitation of early-life thermal acclimation.

2023 SCIENCE OF THE TOTAL ENVIRONMENT

Reactions of juvenile coral to three years of consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Science of the Total Environment, 866, 161227.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Juvenile corals became progressively less resilient to repeated annual heat stress. Survival declined substantially after consecutive exposure, while symbiont density and photosynthetic performance also decreased. The results demonstrate that repeated short heatwaves can have cumulative consequences during vulnerable early life stages.

2022 COMMUNICATIONS BIOLOGY

Adaptations by the coral Acropora tenuis confer resilience to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Kitanobo S, Nakamura T, Morita M* (2022). Communications Biology, 5, 1–10.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

Heat-stressed adult corals produced more eggs but smaller eggs, revealing a trade-off in reproductive investment. Importantly, larvae and recruits originating from heat-stressed colonies showed enhanced survival under elevated temperatures, suggesting that parental thermal history can influence the heat resilience of coral offspring.

2014 SCIENTIA MARINA

Mass coral bleaching in the northern Persian Gulf, 2012

Kavousi J*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Mohammadizadeh M, Bahrami A, Barkhordari (2014). Scientia Marina, 78(3).

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT

The 2012 thermal anomaly produced extensive coral bleaching across the northern Persian Gulf, affecting approximately 84% of surveyed colonies. The study estimated a local bleaching threshold of approximately 33.5–34 °C and demonstrated that even turbid environments did not necessarily protect corals from severe thermal stress.

SCIENTIFIC ASSESSMENT & POLICY

World Ocean Assessment III

Beyond peer-reviewed publications, I contribute to international scientific assessment of the state of coral reef ecosystems and their responses to climate change.

2026 UNITED NATIONS · WORLD OCEAN ASSESSMENT III

Tropical and Subtropical Coral Reefs

Contributing Author

Third World Ocean Assessment (WOA III), Section 4, Chapter 5, Subchapter 5d: Tropical and subtropical coral reefs.

ASSESSMENT HIGHLIGHT

The assessment synthesizes evidence on global coral reef decline, increasing heatwave and disease pressures, the consequences for biodiversity and ecosystem services, and emerging approaches for conservation, restoration and climate adaptation.

The chapter addresses the accelerating pressures facing coral reefs and identifies priorities including standardized monitoring, identification of climate-resistant coral genotypes, improved data integration and stronger evidence-based conservation.
